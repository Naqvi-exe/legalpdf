A Practical Guide to Bates Numbering for Discovery

Published October 1, 2025 · 6 min read

Bates numbering is the standard method for indexing and identifying pages in legal discovery. It provides a unique identifier for every page, making it easy to reference specific documents during depositions, hearings, or trials.

Standard Bates Format

A standard Bates number usually consists of three parts:

  • Prefix: An identifier for the case, firm, or producing party (e.g., "ABC" for ABC Corp).
  • Sequential Number: The unique page number (e.g., "000123").
  • Suffix (Optional): Sometimes used for confidential designations (e.g., "CONF").

A complete example looks like: ABC000123.

Placement Rules

Where should the number go? There is no universal rule, but the most common placements are:

  • Bottom right corner (most common).
  • Bottom center.
  • Top right corner.

It must be legible and not obscure any substantive text. Always check the local court rules or the specific stipulation agreement for formatting requirements.

Applying Bates Numbers Quickly

Traditionally, this required Adobe Acrobat Pro or dedicated litigation software. However, for smaller batches of documents, you can apply Bates numbers directly in your browser.
